# Comfortably Numb studio tone — source notes (2026-10-18)

## Source and method

- **Video:** MustyGuitar, *“Why Comfortably Numb Tone Is IMPOSSIBLE?! (DEEP
  ANALYSIS!)”*, YouTube, 41:26, published 2025-12-14.
  <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TuY9ecHir8E>
- **Captions:** `yt-dlp` English auto-captions, cleaned and de-duplicated. Times
  are approximate (auto-caption cue starts). The captions mishear names; these
  have been normalised where the intended term is clear: “Davy Kilmore” →
  David Gilmour, “Highwatt” → Hiwatt, “Big Mouth/Pig Muff” → Big Muff, “Salt” →
  Sovtek, “Pet Cornish” → Pete Cornish, “SSL 5” → Seymour Duncan SSL-5,
  “Yamaha rotating cabinet” → Yamaha RA-200. Terms still uncertain are marked
  **[uncertain]**.
- **Status:** one player's listening experiment and demo, not a session
  document. Cross-checked below against Guitar.com, Reverb, Gilmourish and Kit
  Rae. Do not treat the video as proof of the 1979 session chain.

## The video's thesis

1. There is no single Comfortably Numb tone: the studio, Wall live, Gdansk,
   Pompeii, Pulse, Delicate Sound, Circus Maximus and 2024 Luck and Strange
   versions all use different modulation and arrangements.
2. The **1979 studio tone is the outlier** — even Gilmour live, with the same
   core gear, does not reproduce it.
3. The missing ingredient is largely the **mix/studio**: compression, EQ,
   reverb, double-tracking, mic choice, desk and the final mix. The raw guitar
   tone alone is not the record.
4. He still believes the studio core was: **Big Muff → MXR digital delay →
   Yamaha RA-200 rotary → Hiwatt(s)**, split dry/wet, plus studio
   reverb/compression. He does **not** hear a flanger or chorus on the studio
   version; he does hear them on most live versions.
5. He is openly unsure about the exact pedals (“maybe he used a different
   Big Muff… maybe a Boss DS-1… maybe he doesn't want to share the spice”).

## Spoken walkthrough (chapter timestamps from the video)

### 00:00–03:09 — why it's “impossible”

- He has tried for months and given up “for now”.
- He listened to roughly 30 live performances and never once heard the studio
  tone reproduced.
- The studio guitar, extracted, “might not sound impressive on its own, but the
  mix…”.

### 03:09–08:06 — 13 isolated performances

This chapter is almost entirely audio clips (the transcript is music cues and
“hey”/“heat”), not spoken advice. It demonstrates the differences; it is not a
source of settings.

### 08:06–14:50 — objection and gear discussion

- Live differences are not just “different ambience”: Pulse has chorus + rotary;
  Gdansk has a Big Muff into an overdrive/tube driver; Pompeii has a flanger;
  Wall live has heavy flanger + Yamaha rotary; Delicate Sound has chorus.
- He argues the 2024 Luck and Strange tour used the same core chain as the 1979
  studio recording — “Big Muff, his MXR digital delays, the blue rack-mount
  thing, his Yamaha rotating cabinet and his Hiwatts” — with dry/wet splitting,
  plus studio reverb/compression.
- He doubts a Colorsound boost was used on the studio recordings (“not even on
  one song”); he associates it with the 1977 In the Flesh tour.
- He does **not** hear a flanger or chorus on the studio version. He says this
  repeatedly and marks it as his ear, not documented fact.
- He notes the 2024 Circus Maximus version, with “the exact same gear,
  relatively”, still sounds different from 1979: different pickups, amp
  settings, rotary mix, pedal settings, mix.

### 14:50–16:02 — his demo setup (not Gilmour's)

- One-watt amp, extremely low volume, gain about 2, one SM57 near the centre.
- Strings are audible over the amp.
- Seymour Duncan **SSL-5** for the non-EMG tones; **EMG** pickups for the
  80s/90s tones.
- No EQ or mix in the demos, just a little Logic reverb at the end.

### 16:02–20:13 — The Wall live (1980)

- Heavy modulation: flanger + Yamaha rotary + delay; on the second leg he says
  Gilmour switched to a Boss CE-2.
- He opens the rotary and adjusts the flanger, using a Ram's Head Big Muff.

### 20:13–23:28 — Live in Gdansk (2006)

- Big Muff into an overdrive/tube driver; darker and smoother than the
  straight Muff.
- He says this is the only time Gilmour used an overdrive **after** the Big
  Muff.
- He tries a Tube Driver clone, lowers gain, opens the tone.

### 23:28–25:59 — Live at Pompeii (2016)

- Big Muff + flanger + delay; the flanger was mixed around 75–80% via a
  parallel mixer.
- **Big Muff starting point:** tone around 11 o'clock, sustain at maximum,
  volume to taste. He then lowers the tone a little.
- He notes this is a good general Big Muff starting point.

### 25:59–27:53 — Circus Maximus (2025)

- Big Muff + rotary; more reverb.
- The low notes are intentionally muffled in that live tone. He says that
  muffled low end is necessary for Circus Maximus but **does not work for the
  studio tone**.

### 27:53–31:46 — attempting the studio album (1979)

- He starts from the Circus Maximus/live chain and removes/changes elements,
  tries a cleaner Big Muff, lowers delay, raises rotary, adjusts tone/sustain.
- Low notes still sound muffled; he concludes the studio tone needs a different
  balance.
- He tries EMG pickups to darken/balance the fuzz; it is “definitely not it”.
- He ends the section unable to reproduce the studio sound. This is the
  video's central admission, not a recipe.

### 32:04–34:18 — Delicate Sound of Thunder (1988)

- Chorus + delay, no rotary; Ram's Head Big Muff.
- He uses “SPC” **[uncertain]** at five/max; the exact pedal/parameter is not
  clear from the captions.
- Conclusion: EMG pickups + Big Muff + chorus + delay gets close to that live
  tone.

### 34:39–38:34 — PULSE (1994)

- Chorus + rotary + delay, dark tone.
- He believes Gilmour used a **Sovtek** Big Muff (caption “Salt”), or possibly a
  Pete Cornish P-2; he uses a P-1 clone.
- He lowers the Big Muff tone because Pulse is dark, maxes a setting, adds
  rotary.
- A Tube Driver/boost may have been used before the Muff **[uncertain]**.
- Final recipe: a smoother Big Muff than the Ram's Head, chorus, delay, rotary,
  high rotary mix.

### 38:54–41:26 — final thoughts

- Two questions remain: why can he get close to the live tones but not the
  studio tone, and why can't Gilmour himself reproduce the studio tone live?
- He suspects the answer is in the mix/studio, not a hidden pedal.
- He plans to revisit with a “Pig Mine” pedal he was sent.

## What this means for our two presets

Current values (both): Hiwatt DR103, WEM 4×12, Big Muff (Ram's-Head voice),
Dyna Comp, Electric Mistress flanger, Echorec delay ~440–450 ms, room reverb.

| Area | Current | Evidence / video | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amp / cab | Hiwatt + WEM/Fane | Matches Guitar.com, Reverb, repo references | Keep; A/B gain only. |
| Fuzz type | Big Muff Ram's-Head voice | Matches the studio sources | Keep. |
| Fuzz settings | tone 0.68 / 0.60, fuzz 0.34 / 0.38 | Video: tone ~0.55, sustain max; Gilmourish: tone ~0.35, sustain 0.70 | A/B matrix; our tone looks brighter than both references. |
| Compressor | Dyna Comp | Matches | Keep; A/B amount. |
| Delay | Echorec, ~440 ms | Digital delay is the studio evidence (MXR DDL or TC 2290); 370 ms per Reverb, 440 ms per Gilmourish's era guide | **High-confidence change:** switch to digital; test 370 vs 440 ms. |
| Modulation | Electric Mistress, mix 0.18–0.20 | Mistress is contested for the studio; the RA-200 is the real studio modulation and is mixed very low; CE-2 is the closest pedal emulation | A/B: Mistress off / low mix, chorus low mix, both. |
| Rotary | none | Yamaha RA-200 is central to the studio sound | Feature gap, not a preset value; chorus is the closest existing stage. |
| Pickups | not modelled | Bridge SSL-1C/SSL-5 or DiMarzio FS-1 depending on source; neck+bridge toggle may have been used | Player-side; mention in the preset description/notes, not a parameter. |
| First vs second solo | both built on the same Muff/flanger recipe | The video treats one tone; the isolated tracks are separate | Research the two isolated solos separately; they likely need different fuzz/compression/level. |
| Studio mix | one preset, no ADT/desk | ADT, console EQ, compression, plate reverb and mic/mix are outside a single preset | Set expectations; aim for the isolated raw tone. |

## First pass — result (2026-10-18)

**Digital-delay swap: shipped.** Both bundled Numb presets changed from
`delay type = 0.5` (Echorec) to `0.0` (digital), with time/feedback/mix
unchanged. The maintainer A/B'd the two user-preset variants and preferred the
digital repeat on both solos: the single clean repeat sits behind the phrase
instead of the Echorec's dark multi-head cluster. The fidelity baseline was
re-blessed.

Offline render notes (synthetic DI, before the bundled change): Solo 1 digital
was 0.76 dB quieter and +7.8% centroid; the outro was 0.37 dB quieter, +0.79 dB
crest and −10.6% centroid. The LTAS shifts reached ±5–6 dB in places, so this
is a real voicing change; the ear test decided it.

## Proposed next experiments

1. **Modulation matrix.** (a) Mistress off; (b) Mistress mix 0.08; (c) chorus
   on, flanger off, low mix; (d) both low. Compare against the isolated first
   and second solos.
3. **Muff matrix.** Tone 0.40 / 0.50 / 0.55 / 0.60; fuzz 0.35 / 0.50 / 0.65;
   level matched for loudness.
4. **Amp gain.** Hiwatt gain 0.34 / 0.40 / 0.48; keep it clean-ish.
5. **Reference analysis.** Use the isolated first/second solo audio from Kit
   Rae (linked by Reverb) for LTAS/centroid/loudness comparison, then ear-test.
   Do not commit the audio to the repo.
6. **Only then** update the two bundled presets, re-bless the fidelity
   baseline if metrics change, and record the sources/caveats in
   `docs/fidelity-references.md`.
